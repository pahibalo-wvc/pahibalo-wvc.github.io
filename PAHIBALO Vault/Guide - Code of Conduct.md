# Code of Conduct

Back to [[Home]]

> [!summary] In short
> The 2025 Student Code of Conduct and Interventions: minor, major and grave infractions, what happens after each, and how to appeal.

## What it covers
The code applies to every PSHS scholar in all school activities: on campus, off campus and online. It also applies whenever you are wearing the uniform or representing the school. Its core values are integrity, excellence and service.

The code expects you to come to class on time, wear the uniform and submit your own work. You also have to follow the rules on AI and other outside help, respect teachers, and use the school's name and logo responsibly. Online, protect other people's privacy. If something puts the community at risk, report it.

## The three kinds of infractions
A minor infraction is everyday misbehavior that disrupts class but does not put anyone at risk. Repeated tardiness, small disruptions in class, improper language and careless handling of school things all count as minor.

A major infraction is deliberate or careless rule-breaking. The code lists damaging school property on purpose, bullying or harassment, academic dishonesty, sexual misconduct, fighting, hacking into school systems or records, gambling, carrying dangerous items, illegal drugs and open disrespect to school authorities. Smoking or vaping, stealing, tampering with fire extinguishers or alarms, and threatening violence are also listed.

A grave infraction puts lives or safety at risk. Examples are alcohol on campus, fights that cause serious injury, guns, explosives or other weapons, and any act that breaks a special law or the Penal Code.

## What can happen
For a minor infraction, a teacher would usually give a verbal or written correction. It can also lead to a talk with your homeroom adviser, a parent-teacher conference, a reflective essay, confiscation or paying for damage, or a restorative activity like community service.

Major and grave infractions get sanctions after the full process below. A sanction can be a written warning, losing privileges or the chance to join school activities, supervised community service, or a referral to guidance. The heaviest sanction is termination of scholarship or non-graduation. This can be recommended after three major infractions during your stay, or after one grave infraction decided by the SCC and approved by the Campus Director.

The school still supports you while you finish an intervention, so you can keep up with your studies.

## How a case goes
1. A staff member sees or receives a report. They tell the Student Conduct Officer (SCO) within one school day.
2. The SCO looks into it within three school days and may ask witnesses for written statements.
3. The SCO decides if it is minor, major or grave. Minor cases get guiding interventions and end there.
4. For major and grave cases, the SCO holds a preliminary inquiry with you and your parents. You get to explain your side and show evidence.
5. The Student Conduct Committee (SCC) holds a formal inquiry. It starts at least five school days after you are notified (unless you agree to an earlier date) and ends within 15 school days. You can bring a representative, like a parent, plus witnesses and evidence.
6. The SCC sends its findings to the Campus Director within five school days. The Campus Director decides, and you and your parents are told in writing.

## Appeals
You can appeal in writing to the next higher authority within seven school days of getting the decision. If you miss this deadline, the decision becomes final. The Board of Trustees, through its Executive Committee, makes the last call on any appeal. While an appeal is pending, the sanction is put on hold, unless someone's safety is involved.

Records of the case are kept in the [[Office - Student Conduct Office]] and shared only with people allowed to see them.
