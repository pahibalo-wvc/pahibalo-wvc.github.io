# Tools

Back to [[Home]]

> [!summary] In short
> Outside apps scholars use for schoolwork: Canva and DaVinci Resolve for design and video, ScienceDirect and Google Scholar for research papers, Desmos for graphs, VS Code for coding and Obsidian for notes.

These are not school systems. They are free or partly free apps from other companies. The school's own systems are on [[Kit - School Online Systems]].

## Canva
A design app in the browser and on phones. Orgs use it for pubmats, posters, certificates and slides. The free plan covers most school work. Canva also runs Canva for Education, which is free for K-12 teachers and their students. Students cannot sign up for it alone: a teacher has to invite them. Whether PSHS-WVC teachers use it is (unknown).

## DaVinci Resolve
A video editor from Blackmagic Design for Windows, Mac and Linux. The free version is enough for class videos, plays and org teasers. It needs a strong computer: version 21 asks for at least 16 GB of memory on Windows.

## ScienceDirect
Elsevier's site for journal articles and book chapters. You can search and read abstracts for free. Open-access articles are free to read in full, but most others need a subscription. Whether the [[Office - Library Unit]] gives scholars access is (unknown).

How to search it well:
- Use Advanced search and put your keywords in the "Title, abstract or author-specified keywords" box. This skips papers that only mention your topic once.
- Put a phrase in quotation marks, like "water quality", and join ideas with AND, OR and NOT in capital letters.
- Filter by Years to keep your sources recent, and by Article type. Review articles sum up a whole topic, so read one first when you are new to it.
- Tick the "Open access & Open archive" filter to see only papers you can read in full for free.

## Google Scholar
Google's free search for research papers, theses and books (scholar.google.com). It searches many publishers at once, including ScienceDirect, so it is a good first stop for your RRL.

How to search it well:
- Use "Since" and "Custom range" on the left to keep results recent.
- Look for a PDF link on the right of a result. If there is none, click "All versions"; another copy may be free.
- "Cited by" shows newer papers that used this one. "Related articles" finds similar papers.
- The quotation-mark button ("Cite") gives the citation in APA, MLA and other styles. Check it against your style guide before you paste it.
- Advanced search (from the menu at the top left) lets you search by author, by journal, or only in titles.

## Desmos
A free graphing calculator in the browser and on phones (desmos.com). Type an equation and the graph appears. Sliders let you change a value and watch the graph move, and you can paste a table of data and fit a line or curve to it, which helps with lab reports. Desmos also has scientific and geometry calculators.

## Visual Studio Code
A free code editor from Microsoft for Windows, Mac and Linux. Add extensions for the language you use, like Python, and you can write, run and debug code in one place. It is handy for computer science classes and for research that needs code.

## Obsidian
A free note-taking app for personal use, on computers and phones. Notes are plain Markdown files saved on your own device, and you can link notes to each other to build your own study wiki. Syncing between devices through Obsidian is a paid add-on, but you can keep the notes folder in a cloud drive instead.
